Cohere Editorial

Future colleague? That’s the hypothesis. The editorial work below is my evidence.

What Cohere helps organizations do, who needs to understand it, and how I’d design content around their decisions.

I conducted an independent editorial assessment of Cohere’s content ecosystem and developed a strategy grounded in Design Thinking, reader journey mapping, and DQTI-informed content quality principles. Through product research, content audits, and audience analysis, I synthesized a framework for technical storytelling, editorial governance, and measurable content effectiveness. The resulting work includes a service map, editorial guidelines, writing samples, and a proposed content roadmap.

How I developed the content strategy

Content strategy is the set of decisions that makes useful information reach the right person, in the right format, at the right moment. For an AI product, that also means ensuring every claim can be traced to evidence and every technical detail has an accountable reviewer. This is my independent, public-source assessment, not an internal engagement with Cohere.

  1. 1. Understand the product and its promises

    I compared public product announcements, technical documentation, customer stories, and research explanations. The first question was not what to publish. It was what each product enables, what is substantiated, and what readers still need to know.

    Explore the product research · Inspect the source audit and evidence coding

  2. 2. Define audiences by the decisions they face

    I mapped a hypothetical route from discovering an AI service to evaluating, implementing, and using it. A business sponsor needs credible outcomes; a developer needs a reproducible test; an IT reviewer needs deployment and permission details. These are working hypotheses, not validated customer personas.

    Open the service map, journey, and example reader personas

  3. 3. Diagnose content quality and gaps

    I applied Design Thinking to frame reader jobs, journey mapping to locate decision points, and DQTI-informed questions about whether information is easy to use, understand, and find. I also checked terminology, claim qualifications, and the handoff between marketing and technical documentation. These are lenses for an editorial assessment, not an official Cohere audit score.

    Review the audit · See how terminology travels across channels

  4. 4. Prioritize and commission the right content

    I translated gaps into editorial opportunities, then weighed audience friction, evidence readiness, claim risk, and timing. The result is not simply a list of articles. It is a sequence of briefs, owners, review gates, distribution paths, and maintenance decisions.

    Read the six-week publishing plan · See a simulated prioritization decision

  5. 5. Govern, measure, and improve

    I proposed a shared vocabulary, verification and editorial review rules, and success measures tied to actual reader tasks. A page is not successful merely because someone clicked it; the stronger test is whether they can find it, understand it, and use it correctly.

Methodological distinction: the published sources and sample edits are real portfolio artifacts. Reader personas, publishing resources, test data, and projected outcomes are illustrative and need validation with Cohere’s internal teams and data.

What the strategy recommends

Make one verified product story work across several moments in a reader’s journey. A launch explains why a capability matters, a decision guide helps readers evaluate it, the technical reference makes implementation dependable, and customer evidence shows what happened under specific conditions. Each format has a different job, but the terminology and claims must agree.

Opportunity

Close the gap between awareness and hands-on evaluation, especially when a release introduces a new technical choice.

Content response

Pair launch storytelling with a task-oriented comparison guide, reference links, examples, and clear fallback behavior.

Operating model

Assign a content owner and a technical reviewer. Keep a claim ledger, version and availability checks, and a review trigger when product details change.

Proof of value

Track whether eligible readers complete the evaluation and where they get stuck. Distinguish content problems from access or product limitations.

My content strategy for Cohere

Help the right person take the next step with enough evidence to act. I’d use one verified set of facts and change how I present them depending on who needs to make the decision.

  1. Make the business use concrete: Use launch stories and customer cases to show the work someone can do, who it serves, and which results the evidence supports.
  2. Make evaluation practical: Give buyers a pilot plan and developers a way to run a comparison. I’d start with retrieval because the launch and the docs leave a concrete question about what to test and what to do when no passage qualifies.
  3. Support rollout and continued use: Connect implementation docs, onboarding, permissions, and troubleshooting. Keep official names, availability, and reviewed claims consistent across those pages.
  4. Measure what the reader can do next: Track qualified interest for launch content, evaluation completion for developer guides, and recurring blockers after rollout. Use that evidence to decide whether to revise the content, change how readers find it, or raise a product issue.

Why I follow Cohere

When I worked on drift metrics at IBM, I kept coming back to the question a reader might ask. What does it mean when this number moves? Cohere Labs’ work on reasoning confidence caught my attention for the same reason. An estimate can help, and there’s still a person who has to decide what to check next.
