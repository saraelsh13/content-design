What Cohere does

Cohere builds AI products for organizations. A company might use them to help employees find answers in its own documents, build an assistant into its software, or run AI models on dedicated infrastructure. An AI model is the software that performs a task such as ranking search results or drafting an answer.

Take an operations team that keeps getting the same question about an internal policy. People need to find the right version, see the source, and know what to do next. Leaders want to know whether the tool saves time. The IT team needs to know what information it can access. Those are three different questions about the same experience.

How the products fit into the journey

A team can start with a workspace for employees or build search and AI into its own software. The choice depends on who will use it, where they work, and how much the organization needs to control.

Give employees a workspace

North gives employees a place to ask questions and use assistants for recurring work. A team could pilot it with one policy library and a few common requests.

Build search into an existing tool

Compass helps find relevant material across company sources. Teams building their own search can also use Embed to support search, Rerank to reorder the results, and a Command model to draft an answer when the workflow calls for one.

Decide how to run it. Model Vault is Cohere’s managed option for running supported models on dedicated infrastructure. The team would check its region, model choices, access, and data-handling requirements for the specific offering.

Who I design the content for

I use these as working personas, then validate them with customer interviews, sales and support questions, and search behavior. Several people may make decisions at the same stage, with different evidence needs.

Business sponsor

Decision. Is this worth piloting, and how will we judge it?

I’d lead with a workflow, who benefits, and a credible customer result. Then I’d offer a pilot brief with an outcome the sponsor can track.

Knowledge or operations lead

Decision. Which team, documents, and tasks should we start with?

I’d give them a pilot checklist with prerequisites, representative questions, and ways to verify the results before rollout.

Developer or technical evaluator

Decision. Can I make this work with the systems and data I already have?

I’d give them a reproducible example, expected results, a fallback for edge cases, and links to the official technical details.

IT, security, or procurement reviewer

Decision. Can we use this within our requirements?

I’d make deployment, permissions, data handling, and availability easy to compare, with links to the relevant documentation and review owners.

Employee using the tool

Decision. Can I use this answer to do my job?

I’d write prompts and help content around common tasks, make the source easy to find, and explain what to try when the result is incomplete.

My content strategy for Cohere

Help the right person take the next step with enough evidence to act. I’d use one verified set of facts and change how I present them depending on who needs to make the decision.

  1. Make the business use concrete. Use launch stories and customer cases to show the work someone can do, who it serves, and which results the evidence supports.
  2. Make evaluation practical. Give buyers a pilot plan and developers a way to run a comparison. I’d start with retrieval because the launch and the docs leave a concrete question about what to test and what to do when no passage qualifies.
  3. Support rollout and continued use. Connect implementation docs, onboarding, permissions, and troubleshooting. Keep official names, availability, and reviewed claims consistent across those pages.
  4. Measure what the reader can do next. Track qualified interest for launch content, evaluation completion for developer guides, and recurring blockers after rollout. Use that evidence to decide whether to revise the content, change how readers find it, or raise a product issue.

Why I follow Cohere

When I worked on drift metrics at IBM, I kept coming back to the question a reader might ask. What does it mean when this number moves? Cohere Labs’ work on reasoning confidence caught my attention for the same reason. An estimate can help, and there’s still a person who has to decide what to check next.
