How I write a customer incident report

At Marqeta, I wrote customer-facing incident reports (CFIRs) to explain what happened, who was affected, how we restored service, and what changed afterward.

Write for someone who just lived through the incident

When I write a customer-facing incident report, I start with the questions the reader is likely to have. Was my service affected? When? Has it recovered? Do I need to do anything about it? Those questions shape the order of the report.

From an incident timeline to a customer report

I used the technical timeline to verify the sequence of events and led with the impact the customer could observe. I distinguished service recovery from follow-up prevention work and called out any next step for the customer.

A reusable structure

SectionWhat I write
ImpactAffected service, customer-visible symptom, scope, and start and end times with a time zone.
Confirmed causeThe failure mechanism in plain language and any contributing detail that helps the reader understand the impact.
ResolutionWhat the response team did, when service recovered, and how recovery was verified.
PreventionFollow-up actions and their status. Separate completed changes from planned work.
Customer next stepState the action, if any. Give a specific support path for customers who still see the symptom.

Illustrative copy, with placeholders

Lead with what the customer experienced

Between [start] and [end] [time zone], some requests to [service] returned [symptom]. We restored [service] at [time] and verified recovery by [verification]. If you still see [symptom], [specific next step].

I use the technical timeline to verify the account and include detection or response details when they answer a customer’s question.

What the customer needs from the report

State the customer impact, explain what changed, and distinguish restored service from work that is still underway.
