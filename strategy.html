Proposed six-week editorial plan

What I’d publish first

Use a shared evidence base to publish across product marketing, developer education, customer stories, and thought leadership.

Reference topicLook up the publishing order, owners, and review gates.

What the content plan needs to do

Cohere’s readers arrive with different goals. A leader wants to see a business use, a developer needs a way to test it, and an existing customer may want help putting it into practice. This proposed six-week plan shows which pieces I’d assign, when I’d publish them, and what each one helps a reader do.

Start with the Cohere overview and journey map

The first editorial decision

I’d start with retrieval because it connects three distinct reader questions Cohere can answer with public evidence. A knowledge lead wants to find the right moment in a recording. A developer wants to decide what passages to pass to a model. An engineering leader wants to know how another team evaluated latency and cost.

Compass Video Intelligence is the time-sensitive piece. I’d prioritize a short design-partner story while the registration link is active. I’d also schedule the Rerank explainer early because its source is ready and it can serve readers beyond launch week. Customer approvals and research review can start in parallel.

With the Content Strategist, validate these questions against site search, search queries, customer calls, and existing pages. For search and answer engines, use a descriptive title, a direct answer near the top, specific nouns, and links to canonical evidence. Keep the same facts in social, email, and article metadata. Review early qualitative feedback, then compare performance with similar pieces once comparable data is available.

From source to publication

  1. Commission: Record the reader, decision, format, evidence, owner, deadline, and preferred next action.
  2. Draft and coach: Use the opening and section order to test the argument. Agree on structural revisions before line edits. Review sentence-level choices together on a short sample.
  3. Verify: Record model versions, dates, permissions, baselines, and approvals in a claims ledger. When two sources disagree, flag the sentence and give the appropriate owner a decision to make.
  4. Publish and maintain: Check links, accessibility, metadata, related pages, and the handoff to distribution. Put an owner and a review trigger on product-sensitive claims. Capture repeated fixes in the style guide.

What the plan protects

Make space for time-sensitive work while keeping an evergreen guide ready if a claim, customer approval, or release date changes.
