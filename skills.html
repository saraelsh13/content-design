How I architect information

Before I write a screen or structure a guide, I work out what someone needs to decide, which system rules apply, and what should carry over to the next project.

User intent

What decision is the user trying to make?

Ask product managers, business analysts, and UX researchers.

Interface requirements

What does the system need from the user?

Check product rules, interface states, and technical requirements with design and engineering.

Friction

Where might someone hesitate, misunderstand, or make an error?

Look at support questions, error states, usability findings, and legal or compliance requirements.

Memory

Which decisions will the team need to use again?

Check existing patterns and past decisions. Record new rules somewhere the team can find them.

  1. AlignAgree on the user decision and the product constraints.
  2. FrameMap intent, requirements, friction, and what the team already knows.
  3. MakeBuild a decision tree, content model, or component pattern.
  4. PlaybackWalk the work through with the team, test assumptions, and record what we decide.

What I make to work through a problem

  • Decision treeTrace the paths a customer can take.
  • Content modelMap the objects, relationships, and states that need consistent language.
  • Component guidanceGive each user question a place in the interface.
  • Content intake and decision logsGather context, record the decisions behind the copy, and prepare for review and translation.
  • Release checks for content patternsConnect intent, product states, accessibility, and localization review.
  • Migration auditFind the tasks developers need to change or verify.
  • Accessibility copy specificationsDocument labels and recovery guidance for each state and language.
  • Documentation navigationOrganize enterprise guidance around reader tasks, including IBM model evaluation.

How I keep the work moving

I’ve set up templates and improved content intake so teams can bring the right information into a request earlier.

I bring product, engineering, design, accessibility, support, marketing, and legal into the decisions they can inform. I check patterns against user feedback, recurring support questions, test findings, or available usage signals. When the same issue keeps coming back, I put the decision into the guide, template, or review process the team already uses.
