Case study · IBM AI ethics

Writing about what an AI system actually did

Research on AI anthropomorphism and guidance for explaining model behavior across interfaces, documentation, and design systems.

What does the verb imply?

As AI features spread across IBM products, teams sometimes used different terms and different degrees of personification for similar behavior. I worked with researchers and AI ethics advocates on terminology guidance and design activities for natural language interfaces.

Illustrative rule

Use a verb that describes the operation

When a system surfaces a match, name the match and the source. When it recommends a next step, make clear who decides whether to take it. A precise verb helps a reader understand where their judgment comes in.

Researching what personifying AI implies

I worked with IBM researchers to develop a mind-mapping tool for content designers. We explored how language introducing an agent before gameplay might shape what people expect it to know and do, and how that framing might affect their understanding of its behavior during a particular interaction and across the system.

Bring the decision to the component

I contributed to conversations and design sessions about how to make the guidance usable across products. We explored how descriptions of an agent shape people’s expectations about what it can do, and how interface language can help someone build a more accurate mental model.

I shared related work at Weavesphere and CASCON. I also wrote about an early AI ethics governance project for IBM Design.
