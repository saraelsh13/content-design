Style guides and content models

I use style guidance to help teams make consistent language decisions, and content models to show where those decisions belong in a product.

Developing the Manulife content style guide

I led content strategy for Manulife’s content design team and developed its style guide. The team used the guide and the design-system component guidance in its work across Bank and Insurance. I brought together terminology, voice and tone, accessibility, localization, and reusable guidance for interface states such as errors, confirmations, and status messages. I also established a decision log so research findings and exceptions could inform the next revision.

Example from my working blueprint

Match the message to what the service confirms

A submission acknowledgement and a completed payment are different states. I modeled each message around its trigger, its component and slot, the localized wording, and the next action the customer can take.

In a companion taxonomy and pilot scorecard, I modeled concepts, labels, journeys, patterns, and placements as separate records. I proposed measures such as critical-state coverage and whether customers can explain the outcome and next step. These were pilot measures, with results still to be collected.

Connect the guide to the way the team works

The style guide recorded the shared rule. The component guidance put that rule where a designer or writer could use it. The decision log gave us a place to capture exceptions and revisit the guide when we learned something new.

From a style rule to a release check

The guide captured the language decisions. In the companion blueprint, I proposed a way to carry those decisions from design through implementation. Here’s how the pattern works for a payment confirmation.

Part of the patternDecision recordedReview check
State and triggerGive submission and completion different message records.Engineering and QA check which service event triggers each message.
Component and placementSpecify the title, supporting text, next action, and announcement behavior.Design and accessibility reviewers check whether people can find and understand the update, including with assistive technology.
Language variantsKeep the English and Canadian French variants linked to the same intent and terminology decision.Localization checks that both versions describe the same state and next step.
Ownership and reviewRecord the rationale, reviewer, and what would reopen the decision.Recheck when the product behavior, terminology, or approval changes.

I proposed piloting the pattern on a small set of critical states. I’d track which states have approved guidance, ask customers to explain what happened and what they would do next, and use those answers to decide where to revise the pattern.

Developing IBM guidance for model behavior

At IBM, I worked with researchers to develop ethical AI language guidance and terminology standards. Teams were describing similar model behavior in different ways across interfaces, documentation, and design systems. I helped turn those differences into guidance writers could apply to a specific action or output.

Illustrative rule based on the portfolio

Name what the system did

When a system returns a match, name the result and its source. When it recommends a next step, make clear who decides whether to take it.

Architecting a content model for mortgage prepayments

At Manulife, I mapped the entities and states the interface needed to name consistently. A customer can submit a prepayment and still need to know whether it has reached their mortgage balance. Giving the payment instruction its own status makes that distinction available across the experience.
