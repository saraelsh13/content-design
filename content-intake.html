Content intake and templates

A request for “confirmation copy” gives me a place to start. I want to know what the person needs to confirm, what the system can verify, and who owns the decision if those two things don’t line up.

I set up intake so stakeholders can bring business rules, technical information, and user evidence into the same conversation. I use what I learn to define the need, draft against actual product states, and give the team one place to review the copy and the decisions behind it.

The intake form

I use a short first pass to understand the request. If it affects money, account access, or another consequential action, I work through the rules and edge cases with the people who own them.

FieldWhat I ask stakeholders to provide
User need and pain pointWho is affected? What are they trying to do, where do they get stuck, and what evidence do we have?
Scope and priorityJourney, channel, audience, regions or locales, release timing, and the impact of leaving the issue open.
Rules and system behaviourEligibility, limits, exceptions, data sources, state transitions, and what triggers each message. Link the relevant policy or technical spec.
Decisions and dependenciesWhat has been confirmed? What is still open? Who can confirm each rule, and which choices affect design, engineering, or another team?
Review and deliveryContent owner, reviewers, required sign-offs, affected locales, and links to the design and delivery ticket.
Acceptance criteriaWhat should someone understand or be able to do, and how will we check it? Agree on a signal to watch after release.

A shared source of truth

I give stakeholders a link to the same working record. It connects the draft to the evidence, business rules, mockups, and technical specs. Each copy entry carries its ID, component or channel, state or trigger, copy type, and current wording. Reviewers can see which version they’re reviewing and leave feedback beside the relevant entry.

I use the decision log to keep what we agreed findable after the comments are resolved. I record the decision, its rationale, the evidence, who confirmed it, the affected copy IDs, and the event or review date that would reopen it.

Illustrative intake and decision log entry

When has an extra payment actually gone through?

Need
I need to know if my extra payment has been applied so I can decide whether to check later or take action.
Decision
Use one message when the request is received and another when the payment is applied. Each message follows its own confirmed event.
Evidence and owner
Link the state model. Ask the product owner and engineering to confirm the events, and record the decision against the two copy IDs.
Acceptance and follow-up
In testing, ask people what happened and what they would do next. Reopen the decision if processing rules change or support keeps hearing the same question.

Copy legend for design and handoff

I label each entry by its job. Designers, engineers, reviewers, and translators can refer to the same copy ID and tell what kind of review it needs.

KeyCopy typeWhat to document
UIHeading, field label, navigation, actionComponent, placement, and the action a person can take.
HELPHelper text, instructions, example, empty stateThe question it answers and when people need it.
ERRValidation and recoveryWhat went wrong, the affected field or action, and a recovery step where available.
STATEProgress, confirmation, success, warningThe verified trigger, what the state means, and whether a next action is available.
A11YAccessible name, description, or announcementHow the text relates to the visible label and when it should be announced.
LEGALDisclosure, consent, or required wordingSource, applicable market, version, and who reviews changes.

Review, translation, and maintenance

  • Assign each review a job. Product and engineering confirm rules and triggers. Content and design check comprehension and placement. Route claims and disclosures to Legal and Compliance where required. Record what was approved and which version it applies to.
  • Send translators the context. Include the copy ID, intended meaning, screen or component, locale, approved terms, and sample values for placeholders. Check the translation in context, including text fit and differences in formats or market rules.
  • Check what people actually encounter. Test errors, accessible names, and relevant status announcements in the build. Confirm the copy matches the system state and the reviewed version. Give recurring support questions, QA findings, or a product change a route back to the content owner.
