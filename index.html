How I architect information

Before I write a screen or structure a guide, I work out what someone needs to decide, which system rules apply, and what should carry over to the next project.

User intent

What decision is the user trying to make?

Ask product managers, business analysts, and UX researchers.

Interface requirements

What does the system need from the user?

Check product rules, interface states, and technical requirements with design and engineering.

Friction

Where might someone hesitate, misunderstand, or make an error?

Look at support questions, error states, usability findings, and legal or compliance requirements.

Memory

Which decisions will the team need to use again?

Check existing patterns and past decisions. Record new rules somewhere the team can find them.

  1. AlignAgree on the user decision and the product constraints.
  2. FrameMap intent, requirements, friction, and what the team already knows.
  3. MakeBuild a decision tree, content model, or component pattern.
  4. PlaybackWalk the work through with the team, test assumptions, and record what we decide.

How I work

I start by asking what a person is trying to do, what the system needs from them, and where the two get in each other’s way. Then I make the decision visible in a flow, a content model, or an example the team can use.
