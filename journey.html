I started in marketing, spent time in a media lab, and ended up explaining AI evaluation metrics and payments APIs. The thread I can see now is a curiosity about how people make sense of complicated things.

Career timeline

  1. University of Guelph

    I started in marketing

    I studied business and marketing. I still start by asking who needs this, what they’re trying to do, and how they’ll know it’s worth their time.

  2. 2018–2019 · Toronto Metropolitan University

    I moved into media and research

    My master’s research used sentiment analysis to map emotional arcs. A UNESCO-supported residency at Hunan University took me into interactive media and design. I also worked on smartphone VR and co-creation prototypes with the Archives of Ontario.

  3. 2020–2025 · IBM Canada Software Lab

    I learned how to write from inside a product

    I worked across enterprise AI and data platforms, from interface language and navigation to model evaluation, installation, and releases. IBM Patterns gave me an early chance to try several design disciplines in the same project. Yes, that’s the one with Rick and Morty.

  4. 2024 · Collaboratory Against Hate

    I studied how people assess credibility

    My co-authors and I studied how people judge the organizations behind fact-checks. It made me more interested in how the source of a claim changes what someone does with it.

  5. 2025–2026 · Financial services

    I worked on decisions with real stakes

    At Manulife I led content design across mortgage, banking, dashboard, and AI assistance journeys. My financial-services work also includes BMO, where I worked on digital banking content.

  6. 2026 onward · Marqeta

    I went deeper into developer experience

    I lead developer content strategy for commercial payments, banking, and money movement. I map integration journeys, write about APIs and incidents, and build ways to spot missing guidance.

  7. 2026–present · University of Toronto

    PhD in Information

    I’m pursuing a part-time PhD focused on human–computer interaction and how people interpret evidence from AI systems.

What I’m researching now

I’m interested in interpretive effort. When an AI system gives us an answer, how much work does it take to figure out what that answer means, what supports it, and whether it’s worth acting on? I’m exploring how human and AI systems use limited information, time, and effort. I’m especially curious about what interfaces can do to help people check a claim.
